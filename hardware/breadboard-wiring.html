Biru Breadboard Wiring

Two buttons on the breadboard

Drawn the way your board sits: USB at the top, 30-pin ESP32 with its pins in columns b and j. Buttons first (wires 1–5): green and orange carry them to D18 and D19; the black ones meet on the left − rail. Then the OLED (wires 6–9) on the ESP32's I²C pins D21/D22. Row shading shows which five holes are joined.

The screen

Push the OLED's 4-pin header into column e, rows 27–30, so each pin gets its own row (it stands upright; face it whichever way you like). Read the pin names off its silkscreen — most are GND VCC SCL SDA, but some swap the first two.

    Black — OLED GND. Row of the GND pin → the left − column.
  2. 7
    Red — OLED VCC. Row of the VCC pin → column a beside 3V3, the very first pin at the top-left.
  3. 8
    Yellow — SCL. Row of the SCL pin → column a beside D22 (14th pin down the 3V3 side).
  4. 9
    Blue — SDA. Row of the SDA pin → column a beside D21 (11th pin down, three above D22).
What to change from the photo

Move two plugs. The black and teal wires on the right side are in the right-hand power rail, which isn't connected to the ESP32. Pull their rail ends out and put them in column a beside D18 (blue button's wire) and D19 (red button's wire). Their button ends can stay.

Check the three left-rail wires share one column. The GND wire and the two button wires on the left must all be in the column next to the blue line. The two rail columns are separate strips — a wire in the + column and a wire in the − column aren't touching.

Check the GND wire is on the 2nd pin. The top-left wire should be beside GND, one hole below 3V3.

Why those two legs

The two legs on the same side (dashed) are already connected inside the switch. Two diagonal legs (green) are the actual switch — open until you press. Using a diagonal pair means it works no matter which way round the button went in.

Reading the board

Each five-hole row (a–e or f–j) is one connection; the centre gap separates the halves. A button leg and its wire just need to sit in the same row on the same side — the shaded rows in the picture.

The ESP32's pins fill columns b–j, so the only free hole beside any pin is column a on the 3V3 side. That's why the buttons use D18 and D19 rather than pins on the other side.

No resistors: the firmware turns on the ESP32's internal pull-ups, so a pressed button simply pulls its pin to ground.
