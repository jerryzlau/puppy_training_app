"Touch" — Biru bops his nose into your flat palm — looks like a party trick, but it's a Swiss army knife. It repositions him without picking him up, ends jumping (nose to palm beats paws on legs), redirects him past scary things on walks, and later powers fancy tricks.

Hold your flat palm two inches from his nose. Dogs investigate — the moment his nose touches, "yes!", treat from the other hand. He'll figure out the game embarrassingly fast; biewers are sharp.

Add the word once he's reliable, then make it mobile: palm a step away, palm low, palm leading him onto the scale at the vet. A dog with a strong "touch" can be steered anywhere, cheerfully.

⚠️ **biewer tip:** being constantly picked up and plopped places is the toy-dog experience — "touch" gives Biru a way to move himself, and dogs with agency are calmer, more confident dogs.
