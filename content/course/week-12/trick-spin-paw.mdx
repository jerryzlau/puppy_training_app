Welcome to dessert. Tricks are pure fun — but they're also disguised training gold: they sharpen his lure-following, deepen his love of sessions, and (research-backed truth) mental work tires a dog out better than an extra walk.

**Spin:** treat at nose height, draw a slow horizontal circle; he follows; "yes!" as he completes it. Shrink the lure to a finger twirl, add "spin!". Train both directions — dogs have a handedness, and one way will be comically harder.

**Shake:** hold a treat in a closed fist at chest height; when he paws at it (he will), catch the paw gently, "yes!", treat. Name it once reliable. The double bill of spin-then-shake is a guaranteed crowd-pleaser and takes about four days total.

⚠️ **biewer tip:** biewers are natural performers with a flair for drama — they learn tricks fast and *remember applause*. Your wife's delighted reaction is a genuine training reward; deploy her strategically.
