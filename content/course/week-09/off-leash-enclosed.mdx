The training-wheels-off moment — in a fully fenced space. Rent-a-yard apps (Sniffspot), a friend's fenced garden, or an empty tennis court all work. Walk the fence line first: a biewer-sized dog fits through biewer-sized gaps, and "enclosed" means enclosed *for four pounds*, not for a labrador.

Then unclip and let him be a dog. Your only jobs: pay every voluntary check-in (he wanders near you unprompted — treat! this is the behavior that makes off-leash dogs safe), and sprinkle in a handful of recalls at moments you'd bet on, each one paid and released back to play.

What you're watching for is the invisible leash: a dog who orbits, checks in, and treats you as home base. That orbit is what you've actually been building for nine weeks.

⚠️ **biewer tip:** keep him leashed anywhere birds of prey are common if he's under 5 pounds — hawks genuinely assess toy dogs. Open rural fields are big-dog territory; enclosed urban spaces are your friend.
