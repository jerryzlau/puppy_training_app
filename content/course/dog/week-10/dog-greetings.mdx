On-leash greetings are dog etiquette on hard mode — leashes trap dogs in face-to-face meetings they'd never choose. Keep them rare, short, and curated.

Protocol: ask the other owner first ("is your dog friendly with small dogs?" — note the specific question). If yes, approach in an arc rather than head-on, allow three seconds of sniffing, then a cheery "let's go!" and a treat for disengaging. Ending greetings *before* things get weird is the whole skill.

Better than face-greetings entirely: the parallel walk. Two dogs walking the same direction ten feet apart, gradually closer, no forced meeting. It's how dog friendships actually form, and it's the safest structure for a 4-pound participant.

⚠️ **biewer tip:** "he's friendly!" shouted from thirty feet, attached to a charging retriever, is not a greeting — it's an incoming event. Step off the path, pick Biru up if needed, zero shame. You're his advocate, not the neighborhood's manners police.
