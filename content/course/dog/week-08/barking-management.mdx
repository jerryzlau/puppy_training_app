Biewers have opinions and a surprisingly big voice for a small chassis. Some barking is legitimate (someone's at the door!) — the goal isn't a silent dog, it's a dog with an off switch.

First, audit: what actually sets him off? Window traffic, hallway footsteps, the doorbell? For window sentries, manage the view (frosted film on the bottom pane is magic). For the doorbell, run the drill: bell rings → treats scatter on his mat → bell *predicts mat snacks*, not intruder panic.

For opinion-barking: acknowledge once ("thank you"), redirect to his mat, and pay the quiet — never yell, which reads as you joining the bark chorus. Reward silence generously; barking that stops working goes extinct.

⚠️ **biewer tip:** small-dog barking gets laughed off until it's a doorbell meltdown at year three. Take it as seriously as you would from a german shepherd — the training is identical, just cuter.
